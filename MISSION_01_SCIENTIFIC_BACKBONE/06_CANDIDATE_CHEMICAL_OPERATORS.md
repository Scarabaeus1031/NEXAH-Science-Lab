# Candidate Chemical Operators

## Disposition

No chemical primitive operator is admitted.

The planetary chemistry study finds recurring observations and process families—condensation, photochemistry, circulation, exchange, escape—but explicitly rejects a shared mechanism or deterministic composition-to-function rule.

## Strong reusable distinctions

- composition is not function;
- shared species is not shared origin or mechanism;
- observed occurrence is not planet-wide causal role;
- accessible atmosphere is not bulk interior;
- phase, reservoir, pressure, altitude, and world must be declared;
- a process family does not imply identical reactions, rates, or parameter regimes.

These are declaration and comparison rules. They do not require new operators.

## Testable hypotheses retained

1. A layered comparison artifact helps readers distinguish composition from function better than isolated planet descriptions.
2. Declared pressure/altitude reference levels reduce misleading giant-planet comparisons.
3. Explicit blocked comparisons reduce deterministic composition-to-property inferences.
4. Condensable roles can be compared across worlds without asserting one universal condensable-cycle mechanism.

## Minimum experiments

### C1 — Composition/function reader test

Compare baseline descriptions with the planetary atlas. Measure whether readers correctly reject `same molecule → same role/outcome` in preregistered cases.

### C2 — Reference-level sensitivity

Recompute comparisons across declared pressure/altitude levels. Treat unstable conclusions as representation dependence, not chemical truth.

### C3 — Process-family expert audit

For condensation, photochemistry, circulation, and escape, require domain experts to record shared variables, non-shared mechanisms, uncertainty, and failure of analogy.

## Admission result

Chemistry supplies domain transformations and comparison cases. Promote no new operator unless a measurement or transformation contract remains distinct after reduction to TRANSFORM, COMPARE, VALIDATE, and representation-specific equations.
