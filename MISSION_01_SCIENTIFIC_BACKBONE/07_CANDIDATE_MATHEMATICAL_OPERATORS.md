## Atlas Q–S–P–J–H–N disposition

The chain is not a mathematical operator algebra. Its source says the layers are conceptual and does not define shared domains/codomains, closure, composition laws, identities, inverses, stability, or error propagation.

Reduce it to:

```text
declare domain and seed
→ REPRESENT partitions and transition structure
→ derive application measurements
→ ORIENT over the resulting Atlas
```

Preserve the original framework as experimental provenance. Mark its operator-theoretic universality conjecture SPECULATIVE.

## Required experiments

### M1 — Gate prospective validation

Freeze estimator parameters on development systems. Predict preregistered transition windows on held-out systems. Compare against density-only, coherence-only, rotation-only, random, and simple trajectory-derivative baselines.

### M2 — DCO discriminant test

Compare DCO against direction change, curvature, local Lyapunov estimates, and shuffled-order nulls. Determine whether it adds information beyond those measures.

### M3 — Representation-loss benchmark

Construct declared forward and reverse representation maps. Measure round-trip inconsistency under representation-specific metrics and relate it to known information loss. Do not call the maps inverses.

### M4 — Typed transition interoperability

Encode one graph path, one IEEE parameterized campaign, one empirical cluster transition, and one censored continuation branch. Test which fields compose and which remain domain-specific.

## Admission result

Retain two research measures. Treat the rest as objects, records, specialized measurements, or compositions of existing primitives. No further mathematical operator is needed now.
