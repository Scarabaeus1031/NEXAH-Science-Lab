## Minimum cross-source meaning that survives

The strongest shared reading is deliberately narrow:

> A Splinter is a localized, typed transition subset or corridor whose
> membership depends on a declared carrier and extraction rule.

The following do **not** survive as general definitions:

- detached physical shard;
- universal cut or vector;
- decision node;
- instability ridge;
- autonomous operator;
- physical Inside/Outside membrane;
- a shape inferred from a wedge-like picture.

## Candidate translations into A0-BL-021

Let

```text
H = 100 oriented two-step histories
A = 56 two-step boundary-slot addresses
C = 56 exact typed terminal classes
Gamma = (A disjoint-union C, H)
```

Three non-identical candidates must be compared rather than conflated:

1. `SPL_H`: the history corridor itself. A history is an edge joining its
   address cell to its terminal class in `Gamma`. This is the closest match to
   the established corridor language, but it is tautological unless compressed.
2. `SPL_LOCAL`: a bounded neighborhood of the two changed seams, represented
   by edge profiles, selected fresh sides, relative boundary positions and the
   local Corner-Flag word around the new vertices.
3. `SPL_DELTA`: the newly added Pascal-face material plus the seam classes by
   which it is attached. This is a local changed subcomplex, but it is not the
   dominant historical meaning of Splinter and requires reconstruction from
   the frozen generator.

The preferred first test is `SPL_LOCAL`: it is non-tautological, extractable
from the declared finite construction and close to the historical transition-
corridor meaning.

## Inside-Out control

`Inside-Out` is not adopted as a physical claim. The available finite control
is an orientation reversal that fixes the selected seam as an unoriented set,
reverses boundary order and swaps `left-radial` with `right-radial`.

Call that provisional involution `J`. The draft test may ask whether

```text
J(J(SPL_LOCAL(h))) = SPL_LOCAL(h)
```

and whether the induced class correspondence is well defined. This remains a
combinatorial reflection/role-exchange control unless a separate embedding
establishes geometric inside and outside.
