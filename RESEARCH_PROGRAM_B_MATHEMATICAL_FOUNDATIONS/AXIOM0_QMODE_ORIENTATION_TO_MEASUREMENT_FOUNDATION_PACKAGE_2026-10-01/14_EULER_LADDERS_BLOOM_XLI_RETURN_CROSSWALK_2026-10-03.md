## Exact XLI bridge

The Meta-Geometrie XXXV-XLI source makes a mathematically controlled distinction.

For a finite carrier `C` and bijection `pi:C->C`:

```text
iteration -> cyclic group
orbits -> disjoint cycles
global inverse -> available
return to identity -> finite order of pi
```

If only a total map `f:C->C` is required and injectivity or surjectivity is dropped:

```text
iteration -> monoid
orbits -> cycles + preperiodic chains + fixed points + attractors
global inverse -> unavailable
state collisions / unreachable states -> possible
```

This is the strongest formal meaning presently available for the Owner's **Bloom** intuition: a transition from reversible cyclic closure to a richer directed functional graph with convergence, merging, transient depth and incomplete return.

## Bloom as a bounded candidate term

`Bloom` may be used in the Core-facing documentation only as a label for a measured increase in iteration structure after a declared rule change. Candidate observables are:

- image deficit `|C|-|f(C)|`;
- number and size of collision fibers `f^{-1}(y)`;
- transient-chain depth before a cycle or attractor;
- number and lengths of cycles;
- indegree distribution of the functional graph;
- loss of globally defined inverse/Return.

Without one of these or another declared measure, Bloom remains visual language. The historical Bloom-to-Tessarna atlas is therefore retained as intuition, not evidence.

## Euler equations and the group/monoid analogy

The analogy becomes useful when carefully typed:

- smooth inviscid Euler evolution is formally time-reversible on an interval where the solution remains regular;
- viscosity gives Navier-Stokes a forward smoothing direction and backward reconstruction is generally not a symmetric operation;
- weak/nonunique solution classes may not define one deterministic evolution map at all;
- a numerical timestepper, a PDE evolution operator and a coarse-grained observation map are different maps and require separate contracts.

Thus `Euler = group` and `Navier-Stokes = monoid` are not adopted identities. The group/monoid plate supplies a diagnostic vocabulary for asking where invertibility, uniqueness and recoverability are lost.
