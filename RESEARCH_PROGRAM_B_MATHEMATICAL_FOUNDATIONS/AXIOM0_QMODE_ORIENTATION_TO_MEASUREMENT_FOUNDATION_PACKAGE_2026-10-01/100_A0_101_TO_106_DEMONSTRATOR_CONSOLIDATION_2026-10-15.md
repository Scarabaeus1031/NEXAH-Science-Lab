## Grid-marker and code-word lens

The Grid Address Reader now replaces its former schematic cyan decoration
with explicitly typed marker layers:

1. **Address / trace:** gold is the current address and cyan is the immediately
   preceding address in the same carrier. Both the full displacement and the
   wrapped `11×11` viewport displacement are reported.
2. **Prime value:** cyan cells are exactly those local values `0…120` that
   satisfy the elementary primality predicate.
3. **Prime fold:** a cell `(c,r)` is marked when `c+r` is prime. This is a
   declared quotient-plus-remainder fold, not an intrinsic property of the
   original integer or carrier.
4. **Code words:** the current integer is shown in base 2 and base 3. The
   displayed substitution `2→x` turns the ternary digit word into the proposed
   alphabet `{0,1,x}` without identifying `x` with an arithmetic operator.

The screenshot pair gives one useful exact contrast:

- `1729 = 15·111+64`, hence `σ111(1729)=15+64=79=p22`;
- `1526 = 13·111+83`, hence `σ111(1526)=13+83=96`, which is composite.

Thus `79` is a prime marker for the declared fold of the first address, not a
universal grid marker. More generally primes become grid markers only after a
predicate such as `prime(cell value)` or `prime(column+row)` has been named.

The atlas therefore keeps two ledgers separate. The **address ledger** retains
quotient `q`, remainder `r`, and the optional declared fold `q+r`. In the
`1729` record neither `15` nor `64` is prime; only their declared fold `79` is
prime. The notation `p22=79` contains one prime value and its ordinal index;
`22` is not a second prime.

The **prime-status ledger** reuses the executed PRIMEGRID Block 2B source:
`1031–1032–1033` has the exact status pattern prime–composite–prime and may be
encoded as `P|C|P → 1|0|1` only after that binary status encoding is declared.
The same source records the exact carry `1032=24·43`. This is a typed ternary
control lane, not by itself a five-cycle or pentagon adjacency.

The reversal pair is an especially useful negative control:
`1087` is prime, while `reverse10(1087)=7801=29·269` is composite. Decimal
reversal therefore preserves the two-element relation/orbit but does not
preserve prime status. The relation ledger and the intrinsic arithmetic ledger
must not be collapsed into one “prime handle.”

The finite word family `Wk=1·0^k·1` yields
