# Foundational thesis

Status: `CONCEPTUAL_RECONSTRUCTION / NOT_A_THEOREM`

## Reconstructed foundation

The historical corpus repeatedly approaches one central problem:

> A physical or formal object is not exhausted by any one representation of
> it, and access to it is conditioned by orientation, selection, projection,
> comparison and measurement.

For current NEXAH purposes, the durable content is not the claim that physics
is false or dispensable. It is the narrower recognition that physical
description does not remove the epistemic and representational conditions
under which observations become comparable.

## Working chain

```text
orientation
  -> distinction and relation
  -> persistence
  -> comparability
  -> projection
  -> measurement
  -> cartography and representation
```

The chain is currently a conceptual dependency proposal. Each arrow needs an
independent definition and, where a necessity claim is intended, an argument
or counterexample program.

## What is retained

- orientation as a declared frame or selection condition;
- distinction and relation as prerequisites for comparison;
- persistence as identity under a declared transformation or observation;
- projection as a possibly lossy representation map;
- measurement as a procedure with an instrument, frame and uncertainty;
- cartography as a record of representation-dependent structure;
- explicit separation of object, representation, observer access and claim.

## What is not retained as established

- orientation as a universal substance or field;
- the automatic emergence of geometry, topology, time or matter;
- observation as a physical creation law;
- one universal observer, coordinate system or Q° center;
- physical necessity inferred from a compelling visual sequence;
- equivalence among philosophical, mathematical and physical uses of the same
  word.

## Relation to the user's stated foundation

The package preserves the broader philosophical motivation that physical
reality need not exhaust reality or intelligibility. Within the public Science
Lab, however, this motivation is translated into bounded scientific questions
about representations, information loss, comparison, measurement and
observer-local access. It is not recorded as an empirical conclusion about
what exists beyond physics.
