## Double pass: same position, different state

On a Keplerian orbit, one radial shell can normally be crossed twice: once on
the outward leg and once on the return leg. A sufficient local state label is

```text
X = (r, sigma)
sigma = sign(dr/dt) in {-1,+1}.
```

Thus

```text
(r,+1) != (r,-1)
```

even though the spatial radius is the same. This makes the supplied phrase

```text
return of position != return of state
```

formally correct. The two colored pass markers are orientation states, not two
different forward-time laws.

## G-ball: “Schweben im Beben”

The G-ball should be treated as a probe whose deviation from an equilibrium
axis is measured. A minimal one-dimensional model is

```text
m*x_ddot + c*x_dot + k*(x-x0) = f(t).
```

Here `x0` is the equilibrium point, `m` the probe mass, `c` damping, `k` local
restoring stiffness and `f(t)` the disturbance. A pendular plate may instead
record an angular deviation `Delta theta(t)`.

“Schweben im Beben” is then the bounded condition

```text
x(t) = x0 + xi(t)
mean(xi) approximately 0
abs(xi(t)) remains bounded over the declared interval.
```

The point holds statistically or dynamically while its trace reveals the
disturbance. A picture of the G-ball does not establish a new gravity law; a
test needs timestamps, units, sensor calibration, forcing/control records and
a null trace.
