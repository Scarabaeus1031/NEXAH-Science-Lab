## SACROPHAG: two-hand enclosure and invisible return

No earlier local source fixes a `SACROPHAG` operator. The owner's new phrase is
therefore registered as a new enclosure grammar with a visible and an invisible

Under one-based prime indexing:

```text

```

The scalar equation says that equal entry and exit shifts cancel. Its more
powerful operator form is a conjugation. Let `T_5` be translation by `+5`, with
inverse `T_5^-1=T_-5`, and let `A_29` be the operation at the `P10=29` core:

```text

```

The two translations are the two hands. One is visible on entry; the inverse
hand guides the return. They enclose the core without being an additional core
state. This precisely formalizes “one hand washes the other.”

The conjugated operator is not generally identical to `A_29`. It is the same
operation expressed in a shifted frame. Conjugacy preserves structural
properties such as cycle structure and operator order, while locations or
coordinates may move. Only when `A_29` commutes with `T_5` does the stronger
identity hold:

```text
T_-5 o A_29 o T_5 = A_29.
```

The alternative word

```text

```

does not close automatically. It has residual `P3-D3` and returns to `P10`
only if the Door term `D3` is explicitly defined to equal or undo `P3` on the
relevant state. This makes the `3p` and `3d` endings experimentally
distinguishable rather than typographic variants.
