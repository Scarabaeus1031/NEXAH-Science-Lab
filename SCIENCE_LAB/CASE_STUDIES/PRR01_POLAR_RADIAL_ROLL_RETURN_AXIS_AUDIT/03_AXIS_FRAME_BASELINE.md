# 03 — Axis and Frame Baseline

In ordinary Euclidean geometry:

- an axis is an oriented/directed reference line once orientation is declared;
- `s` is a coordinate along the axis;
- `r≥0` is perpendicular radial distance from the axis or chosen center;
- `θ` is angular position about the axis;
- angular velocity is `ω=dθ/dt` in a declared frame/time convention.

These roles do not collapse:

`AXIS != OBJECT`  
`AXIS != MOTION`  
`AXIS != SHAFT`  
`AXIS != j`  
`AXIS != RETURN`

A material shaft may be modeled as rotating about its geometric centerline, but the line and the physical member have different identity, material, inertia, and failure conditions. An axis can exist without a shaft; a shaft can bend and have a centerline that changes.

Orientation, angular state, and angular velocity are likewise distinct: declaring an axis does not attest rotation, and declaring an angle does not attest an event.
