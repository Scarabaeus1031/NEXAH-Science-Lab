# 08 — Local Frame / Orthogonality Control

At a regular smooth curve point, the unit tangent is `T=γ'/||γ'||`. With a Euclidean inner product and sufficient nonzero curvature, the Frenet normal `N` and, in three dimensions, binormal `B=T×N` form a local orthonormal frame (M4).

## Boundaries

- the point belongs to the curve;
- tangent/normal vectors live in the relevant tangent/ambient vector space;
- the frame is attached to a point and changes along the curve;
- the normal can require an ambient metric, orientation choice and nonzero curvature;
- a global coordinate frame is a separate choice over the ambient space.

## Standard mathematical role of orthogonality

Under a Euclidean inner product, orthogonal axes make coordinates decouple: vector components are projections, squared norm is the sum of squared orthogonal components, and orthonormal basis changes preserve inner products and distances. This convenience is metric-dependent and mathematical. It makes no claim about architecture, psychology, nature or the universe.
