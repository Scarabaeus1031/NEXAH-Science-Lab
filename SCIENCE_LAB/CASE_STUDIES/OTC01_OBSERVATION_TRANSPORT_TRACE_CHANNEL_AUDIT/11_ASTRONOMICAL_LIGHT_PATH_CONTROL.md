# Astronomical Light-Path Control

This is a standard-optics structural control, not an astronomy program.

| Object | Visible-light role in this control | Path effects before detection |
|---|---|---|
| Sun | Self-luminous source | Absorption, scattering and refraction through atmosphere |
| Star | Self-luminous source | Propagation plus atmospheric refraction/turbulence/scintillation |
| Moon | Mainly reflector of sunlight | Source illumination, surface reflection, propagation and atmosphere |
| Local lamp/flame | Local self-luminous source | Local medium/path, optics and detector |

NASA states that moonlight is reflected sunlight. NOAA documents apparent
displacement near the horizon from atmospheric refraction. NASA documents
scintillation produced by turbulent refractive-index inhomogeneities. NOAA
documents wavelength-dependent atmospheric scattering.

These controls establish:

- emission is not reflection;
- apparent solar position near the horizon need not equal the geometric position;
- stellar intensity fluctuations at the detector can be path-induced;
- spectral/brightness changes can be introduced by scattering and absorption;
- the Earth's atmosphere is not literally a Schlieren instrument.

The shared structure is source–path–transformation–detection. No common physical
mechanism across all OTC-01 domains is claimed.
