# 06 — Established Baseline and Prior-Art Boundary

## Purpose

This record prevents the TOP bookkeeping arrangement from being presented as
new optical physics.

## Established components

### Edge response and imaging metrology

Knife-edge measurements and edge-spread functions are established methods for
characterizing an imaging system and deriving point/line-spread or modulation
transfer information. TOP-BOUNDARY-01 reuses the edge as a controlled
measurement object; it does not claim to invent edge-response metrology.

Reference:

- NIST, edge-spread/knife-edge use in optical-system characterization:
  <https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=930903>

### Aperture functions and Fourier optics

The far-field diffraction pattern of a slit arrangement is determined from
the aperture function through established Fourier optics. Width, spacing and
number of slits are standard physical variables.

References:

- University of Texas, one-dimensional Fourier optics:
  <https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/315/Waveshtml/node95.html>
- University of Maryland, fixed double-slit demonstration with varied widths
  and separations:
  <https://lecdem.physics.umd.edu/m/m1/976-m1-11.html>

### Finite-width double slit

The double-slit record combines the finite single-slit diffraction envelope
with two-aperture interference. A nonzero joint-versus-separate intensity
residual is therefore not, by itself, anomalous.

Reference:

- OpenStax/University Physics, finite-width double-slit diffraction:
  <https://pressbooks.online.ucf.edu/osuniversityphysics3/chapter/double-slit-diffraction/>

## Candidate methodological contribution

The only candidate contribution tested here is the disciplined arrangement of
already valid domain methods in one auditable record:

```text
exact source and apparatus binding
+ full orientation series
+ mask/complement pairing
+ separate/joint-boundary comparison
+ explicit residual maps
+ I-L-A-U and return
+ fixed claim ceiling
```

Whether that arrangement improves reproducibility, error localization or
reader understanding is not answered by the optical execution alone. It would
require a later comparator study against a strong conventional laboratory
protocol receiving the same information.
