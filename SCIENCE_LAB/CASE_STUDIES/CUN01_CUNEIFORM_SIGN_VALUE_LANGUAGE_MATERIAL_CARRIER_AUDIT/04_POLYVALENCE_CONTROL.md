# 04 — Polyvalence Control

## Bounded examples

- DCCLT's MI example assigns one sign form several documented Sumerian lemmas/readings: `ĝi₆` “night,” `ku₁₀` “dark/darkness,” and `giggi` “black,” with separate pronunciation glosses and Akkadian translations.
- The Oracc Sign List entry for AN records `an` “sky,” `diŋir` “deity,” divine-name uses and Akkadian `ilu` “god,” among other corpus-linked values.
- DCCLT and the Met's Old Babylonian exercise show IB/IP syllabic readings and a use for the divine name Urash, disambiguated by a divine determinative.

## Decision

```text
ONE_SIGN_EQUALS_ONE_SOUND=NO
SIGN_FORM_EQUALS_SIGN_VALUE=NO
SIGN_FORM_EQUALS_WORD=NO
SIGN_VALUE_EQUALS_MEANING=NO
POLYVALENCE_SUPPORTED=YES
CONTEXT_REQUIRED_FOR_READING=YES
```

The conclusion is limited to documented sign-list and exercise evidence. It does not assert that every sign has every function or that all periods share identical values.
