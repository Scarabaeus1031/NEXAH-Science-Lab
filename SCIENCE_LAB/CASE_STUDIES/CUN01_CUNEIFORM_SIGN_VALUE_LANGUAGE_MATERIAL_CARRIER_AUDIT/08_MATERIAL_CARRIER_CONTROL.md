# 08 — Material Carrier Control

The Met documents cuneiform as wedge-tipped stylus impressions made in clay. Penn separates elementary wedge production, combinations of wedges and meaningful signs. These observations require at least four layers:

```text
CLAY TABLET / OTHER CARRIER
    != WEDGE IMPRESSION
    != INSCRIPTION / SIGN SEQUENCE
    != SIGN IDENTITY OR FORM
    != READING
```

A carrier may preserve an inscription, but it is not the inscription. A physical mark participates in a sign form under a documented writing convention; it is not by itself a lexical reading.

```text
MATERIAL_CARRIER_DISTINCT_FROM_INSCRIPTION=YES
MATERIAL_FORM_IMPLIES_SIGN_MEANING=NO
```

Tablet, cone and seal-related examples are retained only with their museum-documented functions. No meaning is inferred from object shape.
