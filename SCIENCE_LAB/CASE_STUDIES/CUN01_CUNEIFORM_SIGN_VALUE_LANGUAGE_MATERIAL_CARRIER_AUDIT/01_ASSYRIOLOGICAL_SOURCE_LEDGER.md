## Sources

| ID | Authority | Evidence used | Class | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | [DCCLT — Introduction to Cuneiform Sign Lists](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/dcclt/signlists/SignLists/index.html) | Cuneiform uses logograms and syllabic signs; most signs can write multiple words and/or syllables; context resolves values; Sumerian and Akkadian use substantially different mappings. The MI example records multiple Sumerian lemmas/readings and Akkadian translations. | A | Does not universalize a sign value across languages or periods. |
| S2 | [DCCLT — What is a Lexical List?](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/dcclt/intro/lexical_intro.html) | Lexical/sign lists, bilingual Sumerian–Akkadian practice, scribal curricula, determinatives and a bounded first-millennium hermeneutic example. | A | Does not equate ancient practice with modern linguistic theory or a universal worldview. |
| S3 | [Oracc Sign List — AN](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/osl/signlist/l0065/o0000099/index.html) | Corpus-linked values include Sumerian `an` “sky,” `diŋir` “deity,” divine-name uses and Akkadian `ilu` “god.” | A | Sign-list values require language and context. |

| S5 | [CDLI — Cuneiform Digital Library Preprint 2](https://cdli.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/articles/cdlp/2.0.pdf) | Syllabic signs, word signs and determinatives; determinatives are described as unpronounced logograms that aid value selection. | A | Bounded technical description only. |
| S6 | [Met — Student exercise tablet](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/321878) | Old Babylonian clay exercise; divine indicator plus an IB/IP/Urash sign; signs render words and sounds and can have multiple values. | A | One exercise does not define all cuneiform practice. |

| S9 | [Met — Proto-cuneiform tablet](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/329081) | Wedge-tipped stylus impressions in clay; carrier, impression and recorded information are materially distinct. | A | Material shape alone does not determine linguistic value. |

## Historical claim decisions

| Claim | Class | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Cuneiform sign forms may carry multiple context-dependent values. | A, S1–S6 | Supported. |
| Cuneiform was used for Sumerian and Akkadian with different language-specific mappings. | A, S1–S2, S11 | Supported. |
| Determinatives can supply semantic classification without ordinary pronunciation. | A/B, S4–S6 | Supported within cited practice. |
| Lexical/sign lists documented signs, readings, lemmas and translations. | A, S1–S2 | Supported. |
| Some first-millennium scholarship used syllabic/logographic reinterpretation in hermeneutic texts. | A, S2 | Supported only as the cited bounded practice. |
| Wedge orientation/composition contributes to sign formation. | A/B, S9–S10 | Supported. |
| Wedge directions encode universal or cosmic axes. | D/E | Not supported. |
| Cones encode a fundamental geometry of thought. | D/E | Not supported. |
| Cuneiform contains NEXAH or an operator language. | D/E | Rejected as evidence. |

`VISUALLY_PLAUSIBLE != HISTORICALLY_ATTESTED`, `PHONETICALLY_PLAUSIBLE != HISTORICALLY_ATTESTED`, and `MODERN_ANALOGY != ANCIENT_CONCEPTUAL_IDENTITY` remain mandatory.
