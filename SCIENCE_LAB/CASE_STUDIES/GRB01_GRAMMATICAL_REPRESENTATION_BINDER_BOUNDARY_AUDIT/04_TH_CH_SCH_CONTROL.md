# 04 — TH / CH / SCH Control

## English `th`

Cambridge describes `th` as a digraph. *Thin* is `/θɪn/`, while *this* is `/ðɪs/`. The two characters do not contribute their ordinary independent `/t/` and `/h/` values in these examples, and the sequence has more than one pronunciation.

`TH_STATUS=ENGLISH_DIGRAPH_WITH_CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_VOICELESS_AND_VOICED_VALUES`

## German `ch`

Duden describes front “Ich-Laut” and back “Ach-Laut” variants for the sound represented by `ch`. The sequence remains undivided in word division when it represents a simple sound. Isolated letter names do not predict the contextual realization.

`CH_STATUS=GERMAN_CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_DIGRAPH_OR_LETTER_SEQUENCE`

## German `sch`

Duden explicitly contrasts the three-letter sequence `sch` with one corresponding sibilant, and its word-division rules keep it together when it represents that simple sound.

`SCH_STATUS=GERMAN_TRIGRAPH_OR_COMPOSITE_SYMBOL_FOR_ONE_SIBILANT`

These are language-specific orthographic relations. `th`, `ch` and `sch` are not declared the same structural object across languages.
