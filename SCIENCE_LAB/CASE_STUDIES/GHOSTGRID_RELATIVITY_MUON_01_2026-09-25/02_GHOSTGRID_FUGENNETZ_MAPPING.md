## Minimal typed model

Let \(X=(E_0,E_1)\) be the two-event physical structure: muon creation and a
selected later detection event. Let \(Q_A\) be the Earth observation frame and
\(Q_B\) the muon rest frame.

\[
R_A=\Pi_A(X),\qquad R_B=\Pi_B(X).
\]

The seam between the records is not a visual resemblance. It is the declared
Lorentz map

\[
\Lambda_{AB}:R_A\rightarrow R_B.
\]

The inverse seam \(\Lambda_{BA}=\Lambda_{AB}^{-1}\) returns coordinates when
the complete required record and domain are retained.

## A–B–C to D–E–F–G

The earlier visual grammar remains useful if its types are explicit:

- `A`, `B`, `C`: three declared records or observer frames;
- `D`, `E`, `F`: pairwise comparison / transformation objects;
- `G`: a separately defined common invariant or joint reconstruction result.

For the minimal muon case only `A`, `B` and their seam are physically needed.
`C` may be a comparison representation that displays both, but it is not an
absolute observer and not automatically a third inertial frame. `G` must be
computed from declared invariants; it is not inferred from the visual overlap
of circles.

## Fugennetz claim

Within this case, “relations form the grid” has a precise bounded meaning:

```text
nodes  = frame-bound records and identified events
edges  = typed coordinate transforms or provenance relations
labels = retained invariants, losses, assumptions and uncertainty
```

The grid is therefore a comparison structure over physics records. It is not
the physical field, the cause of time dilation, or a replacement for
Minkowski spacetime.
