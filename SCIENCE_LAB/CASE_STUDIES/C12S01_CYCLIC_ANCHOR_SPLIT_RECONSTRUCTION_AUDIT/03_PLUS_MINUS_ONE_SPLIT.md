# Plus/Minus One Split

## Formal split

For a declared anchor `a` in `Z/12Z`, define the local signed neighborhood:

`N(a) = (a - [1], a, a + [1])`.

At `a = [0]`, this is `([11], [0], [1])`. A linear display may render it as `-1 | 0 | +1`, while a twelve-hour clock may render it as `11 | 12 | 1`.

## What the split establishes

- cyclic adjacency;
- signed displacement relative to a chosen anchor;
- wraparound at the representational boundary;
- recoverability of predecessor and successor from the modulus and anchor.

## What it does not establish

- that 12 is an intrinsic center rather than a chosen label;
- that negative and positive residues are physical domains;
- that the anchor is a portal, observer, zero-node, or imaginary axis;
- that `I` means `1`;
- that the split generates `6+I+6`, prime mirrors, the golden angle, or any historical visual.

## Historical classification

`HISTORICAL_PLUS_MINUS_ONE_ATTESTATION = C_COMPATIBLE_BUT_NOT_ATTESTED`

The source contains the ingredients `12 sectors` and `11–12–13`, but not the explicit signed-offset rule. The formal split is reconstructable; its attribution to the historical author would be an inference.
