# External Test 01 — Strategic Refinancing / Competitive Benchmark

Date: `2026-09-27`  
Status: `CLOSED_EXPLORATORY_EXTERNAL_TEST / METHOD_HYPOTHESIS_PARTLY_SUPPORTED`  
Authority: `ANONYMIZED_TEST_RECORD / NO_FINANCIAL_ADVICE / NO_ACTIVATION`

## Mission

Test whether the NEXAH idea can be exercised against existing public AI and
review platforms: compare answers to one consequential decision, reconcile
their data, timelines and assumptions, locate the boundary of justified
agreement and return an auditable Decision-Assurance statement.

The case is a normalized strategic refinancing scenario. It contains no names,
addresses, organizations, email addresses or source documents. The retained
record is deliberately smaller than the original conversation and preserves
only durable method and competition findings.

## What was tested

1. One realistic but normalized prompt was supplied to several externally
   available platforms and to a NEXAH/Codex analysis.
2. The returned recommendations were compared for access, usability,
   delivery, evidence discipline, timeline, calculation model, assumptions and
   boundaries.
3. Apparent recommendation consensus was separated from differences caused by
   incompatible debt and refinancing models.

This is simultaneously an exploratory external test, a competitive benchmark,
a method experiment and a competition scan. It is not a controlled accuracy
benchmark because no preregistered ground truth, blind evaluator or archived
raw-output corpus was bound before the test.

## Decision

The broad proposition “compare several AI answers” is not a sufficient NEXAH
differentiator. It is already available or easily imitated.

The narrower proposition remains plausible and was partially demonstrated:

> NEXAH reconciles competing outputs onto declared data, time axes and model
> assumptions; reproduces material calculations; identifies which assumption
> causes a divergence; searches decision boundaries; and records what remains
> undecidable.

In this single case, the most consequential divergence was not the verbal
recommendation but the debt amount carried to the refinancing horizon, the
share refinanced and the treatment of the old-rate remainder. The NEXAH/Codex
return made those choices and several calculation boundaries more inspectable.
Its proportional allocation assumption remains unverified against actual
repayment schedules and therefore is not ground truth.

## Claim ceiling

Supported:

- the same normalized decision prompt produced similar high-level advice but
  materially different calculation models;
- hidden timeline and debt-base assumptions can change stress results while
  leaving the prose recommendation apparently unchanged;
- a reconciliation and audit layer is a credible NEXAH product hypothesis;
- external offerings overlap substantially with the broad comparison idea.

Not supported:

- that NEXAH or any compared platform was generally more accurate;
- that the NEXAH calculation was correct for a real portfolio;
- product-market fit, customer demand, willingness to pay or external Human
  utility;
- professional financial advice or an authorized real-world action;
- a general vendor ranking based on one post-hoc case.

## Evidence basis

The controlling input is the Human Owner's anonymized comparison summary. Raw
platform transcripts, product versions, timestamps and original financial
records are not part of this package. CrossChecked produced no usable full
report after two delivery attempts; that is retained as a delivery and
transparency observation, not as evidence of analytical inferiority.
