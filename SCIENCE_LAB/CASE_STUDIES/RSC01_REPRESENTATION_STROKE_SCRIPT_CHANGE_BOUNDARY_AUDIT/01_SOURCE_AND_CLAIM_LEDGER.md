# 01 — Source and Claim Ledger

Only the evidence below is used. Interpretive or civilizational claims are excluded.

| ID | Authority | Bounded evidence used | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | [Asian Art Museum — Chinese Character and Brushstrokes](https://education.asianart.org/resources/an-introduction-to-chinese-character-and-brushstrokes/) | Characters comprise ordered strokes; pressure/lift and ink supply affect thickness, lightness and darkness; dry tips can yield separated parallel traces. | Educational museum source; used only for material/process distinctions, not philosophy. |
| B2 | [Asian Art Museum — Chinese Brushpainting Techniques](https://education.asianart.org/resources/an-introduction-to-chinese-brushpainting-techniques/) | Speed, pressure, moisture, angle and bristle contact alter a brushstroke; multiple ink gradations can occur in one stroke. | Painting examples support production typing only; they do not establish character readings. |
| B3 | [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Chinese Calligraphy](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/chinese-calligraphy) | A flexible hair brush, its tip/side, speed, pressure and ink loading produce variable traces; established stroke order constrains character production. | Curatorial account; expressive commentary is not used as cognitive evidence. |
| B4 | [Smithsonian, National Museum of Asian Art — *Faith & Form*](https://archive.asia.si.edu/exhibitions/online/faithandform/essays.pdf) | A knowledgeable viewer can follow some brush movement and pauses from prescribed sequence and visible ink trace; several strokes may be produced without lifting in running style. | Supports partial trace evidence, not exact reconstruction of every event. |
| V1 | [Cambridge University Press — Romanized writing of Vietnamese](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/language-learning-and-teaching-in-missionary-and-colonial-contexts/42E20998E45385DDC0E58C36CCEFC3B8/listing) | Vietnamese history includes sinograms, Nôm adapted to Vietnamese, and Romanized Quốc Ngữ; the chapter bounds Quốc Ngữ historically from 1615 and notes official status since 1945. | No simple one-step replacement narrative or language-identity claim. |
| V2 | [Vietnamese Nôm Preservation Foundation / National Library of Vietnam project](https://www.nomfoundation.org/nom-project/Digital-Library-of-Han-Nom/Project-Overview?uiLang=en) | Chữ Nôm represented Vietnamese speech and has an attested documentary span from the 10th into the 20th century. | Project overview gives broad chronology; individual character analyses require separate attestation. |
| V3 | [Bảng Tra Chữ Nôm introduction](https://www.nomfoundation.org/nom-tools/BTCN/Introduction?uiLang=en) | The scholarly lookup distinguishes Nôm readings from Sino-Vietnamese readings, supplies textual examples, notes non-standardized spellings, and gives a sourced phonetic-plus-semantic construction example. | One example cannot establish a universal Nôm construction rule. |
| V4 | [Unicode Standard 17.0, Chapter 7](https://unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-7/) | Modern Vietnamese uses vowel letters and five tone marks; one rendered unit can be represented precomposed or as a base plus combining marks. | Encoding structure is not identical to linguistic unit structure. |
| K1 | [Sidnell & Shohet, *Journal of the Royal Anthropological Institute*](https://rai.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1467-9655.12053) | Vietnamese commonly uses kin terms for second-person reference to relatives and non-relatives; forms select relative age or generation, including `anh` versus `em`. | Peer-interaction analysis; not a complete kinship lexicon. |
| K2 | [Lê, *Variation in Linguistic Politeness in Vietnamese*, ANU](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/10093/1/Le_VariationLinguistic2013.pdf) | Kin terms can be self-reference/address forms; examples show `ông ngoại` and `bà nội` as first-person reference, and note contextual/regionally variable use and possible omission of lineage modifiers. | Examples are discourse-bound; no universal fixed politeness ranking is inferred. |
| K3 | [Tokyo University of Foreign Studies — Vietnamese kinship names](https://www.coelang.tufs.ac.jp/mt/vi/gmod/contents/explanation/014.html) | `bà ngoại` denotes maternal grandmother and `bà nội` paternal grandmother; `ngoại` and `nội` identify maternal versus paternal side in this construction. | No symbolic interpretation is attached to spelling, sound or literal spatial glosses. |
