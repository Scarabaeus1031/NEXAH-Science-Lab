# 06 — CONTENT / CONTEMPT Control

## CONTENT

English `content` is one orthographic string with multiple documented lexical readings. Cambridge distinguishes the information/material noun (initial stress; US `/ˈkɑːn.tent/`, UK `/ˈkɒn.tent/`) from satisfied-related adjective/verb and related noun readings (`/kənˈtent/`). Grammatical role, stress, pronunciation and meaning are therefore required to select a lexical item.

Merriam-Webster records historical details for the relevant entries. VOR-01 does not infer whether readings are related merely from their spelling.

`CONTENT_CONTROL=SAME_STRING_MULTIPLE_LEXICAL_READINGS_CONFIRMED`

## CONTEMPT

Merriam-Webster documents `contempt` as `/kənˈtem(p)t/`, meaning despising or lack of respect, with history through Latin *contemnere*. Its visual/phonetic proximity to `content` does not make it the same lexical item, meaning or etymon; the `content` histories cited above derive through Latin *continēre*.

`CONTEMPT_CONTROL=SIMILAR_STRING_DISTINCT_LEXICAL_ITEM_AND_ETYMOLOGY`

## Boundary

```text
SAME_STRING != SAME_PRONUNCIATION
SAME_STRING != SAME_LEXICAL_READING
SAME_STRING != SAME_MEANING
SIMILAR_STRING != SHARED_ETYMOLOGY
```

This is the required homograph/form-similarity control and is not expanded into a catalogue.
