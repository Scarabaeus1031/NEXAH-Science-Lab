# Navigator Utility Test and Scoring Rubric v1

Status: `PREREGISTRATION DRAFT / NO HUMAN STUDY ACTIVE`

## Question

Does the Master Navigator improve accurate orientation across the NEXAH
corpus without increasing false closure or claim inflation?

## Conditions

Each participant completes equivalent tasks in counterbalanced order:

1. filesystem/search baseline;
2. current Mission Control documents;
3. Master Navigator.

The public-reader cohort must not receive internal paths or Mission Control
state. A small internal pilot may precede an independent public-reader test.

## Five tasks

1. Find one HTML artifact's source, module, surface role and claim ceiling.
2. Explain Q7 → Q11 → Tessarec and state what the connection does not prove.
3. Distinguish a historical Polar-Janus PASS from its current interpretation.
4. Find alternate views of one module and identify the current master surface.
5. Decide which candidate is safe for public presentation and name the
   missing gate for one rejected candidate.

## Scoring per task

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | wrong object/source or materially inflated claim |
| 1 | partly correct but misses provenance, relation type or boundary |
| 2 | correct object, source, relation and claim boundary |

Maximum accuracy score: `10`.

Separately record:

- completion time;
- source-selection errors;
- false-promotion errors;
- missed relations;
- confidence from 1–5;
- cognitive load from 1–7;
- free explanation before showing the expected answer.

## Primary endpoints

1. accuracy score;
2. false-promotion rate;
3. median task-completion time.

Secondary endpoints are confidence calibration, cognitive load and qualitative
explanation quality.

## Proposed success thresholds

Thresholds must be frozen before participants see the Navigator:

- median accuracy at least `8/10`;
- at least `30%` faster median completion than filesystem/search;
- source-selection accuracy at least `90%`;
- false-promotion rate no greater than `5%` and not worse than either baseline;
- at least `80%` of participants correctly explain one `does_not_imply`
  boundary without prompting.

Failure of the speed threshold with strong accuracy is a UX repair signal, not
a scientific failure. Failure of the false-promotion threshold is a hard stop
for public release.

## Sample and interpretation

- internal formative pilot: 3–5 users or repeated blinded task sessions;
- independent public-reader pilot: minimum 8 participants from at least two
  reader profiles;
- report individual and aggregate results;
- do not generalize beyond the selected tasks, corpus cut and participants;
- do not call the Navigator validated before an independent pilot passes.

## Required retained artifacts

- frozen task set and answer key;
- participant-information and privacy record;
- counterbalancing schedule;
- anonymized task results;
- analysis script or transparent calculation sheet;
- deviations and exclusions;
- signed Human Owner interpretation and claim ceiling.
