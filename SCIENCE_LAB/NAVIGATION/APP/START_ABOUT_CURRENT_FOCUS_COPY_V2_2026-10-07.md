# B. About

## B1 — Opening

**Kicker**

About the Science Navigator

**Headline**

A map of research relationships—not a claim engine.

**Copy**

The NEXAH Science Navigator is an internal research instrument for exploring
relationships among mathematical models, visual experiments, formal tests and
evidence records. It makes the research landscape inspectable without turning
proximity, analogy or shared vocabulary into proof.

## B2 — Why it exists

**Headline**

Finding a pattern is not yet understanding a transition.

**Copy**

Complex systems can display recurring forms: boundaries, crossings, phase
drift, return structures and possible paths. These forms may be useful to
compare. Visual similarity alone cannot establish a common mechanism.

The Navigator therefore preserves both sides of a connection: what can be
compared and what the comparison does not imply.

## B3 — How it is organized

**Headline**

Framework → families → objects → evidence.

**Framework**

The exploratory roof: shared questions, declarations and claim boundaries.

**Families**

Functional lenses for recurring kinds of relational work—not universal
mechanism classes.

**Objects**

Concrete systems, models, instruments, visual works and source records that
retain their own mathematics and provenance.

**Evidence**

Package-local results, current interpretations, negative controls and maturity
states shown without transferring validity between domains.

## B4 — What it does and does not do

| The Navigator does | The Navigator does not |
|---|---|
| orient across governed records | certify a universal theory |
| expose typed connections and their limits | turn resemblance into proof |
| launch registered instruments | activate a research or runtime profile |
| retain negative and no-result states | rewrite package-local verdicts |
| show public-admission gates | authorize publication |

## B5 — Responsibility map

### Mission Control

Governs currentness, activation, authority and portfolio priority.

### Science Lab

## B6 — How to read the language

**Headline**

Meaning first. Vocabulary connected. Codes retained.

The Navigator begins with a plain-language function, connects it to established

# C. Vocabulary — first release set

## Unified Transition Geometry · UTG

**Meaning**

An exploratory framework for describing boundaries, transitions, directions,
fields and returns across distinct systems.

**Adjacent vocabulary**

Dynamical systems, event surfaces, transversality, return maps and state-space
analysis.

**Does not mean**

A validated universal theory or evidence that different systems share one
mechanism.

## OIL/RIS

**Expansion**

Outer orbit · inner orbit · link/transfer orbit; rotation · inversion · shift.

**Meaning**

A source-bound visual kinematic grammar for several orbit-like views and three
named transformations.

**Does not mean**

Orbital physics, a recovered executable composition or one universal machine.

## Modular Redundancy Binder · MRB

**Meaning**

The inherited name of a numbered audit series beginning with a bounded modular
redundancy and Chinese-remainder test. Later cases extend to mirrors, radix,
return, zipper and Pascal-thread representations.

**Does not mean**

That every MRB case tests redundancy or establishes a new theorem.

## ACR series

**Meaning**

A numbered sequence of closure, representation, compatibility, readout,
projection, reconstruction and audit records.

**Naming note**

ACR-27 is explicitly titled `Angle Closure & Representation Audit`. A single
controlled expansion for the complete later series has not yet been located.
Use each record's functional title.

## Typed split/cut · ø

**Meaning**

Inside the declared Multi-Lens fixture, `ø` partitions the complete source into
two disjoint branches whose union reconstructs the source.

**Does not mean**

The empty set `∅`, an intrinsic universal glyph meaning or an undeclared
operator on another carrier.

## Registered seam/transition · œ

**Meaning**

Inside the declared Multi-Lens fixture, `œ` joins registered views that share a
compatible address and overlap.

**Does not mean**

A standard mathematical operator or an intrinsic meaning outside the fixture.

## Claim ceiling

**Meaning**

The strongest statement a record may support within its declared scope.

**Does not mean**

A confidence score, decorative disclaimer or permission to transfer a result
to another domain.

## Carrier

**Meaning**

The declared object on which a representation, measurement or operator acts:
for example a finite state set, signal, graph, manifold or dataset.

**Does not mean**

That two carriers are identical because similar operator names are used.

## Projection

**Meaning**

A mapping from a source into a reduced, selected or visible view.

**Boundary**

A projection may be many-to-one and therefore lose information.

## Reconstruction

**Meaning**

The attempt to recover a declared state or relation from one or more views.

**Boundary**

Recovering the tested relation does not necessarily recover the complete
source.

## Connection Family · CF:F1–F7

**Meaning**

One of seven functional lenses used to organize recurring relational work.

**Does not mean**

A universal mechanism class or the historical HTML shelf namespace `HL:*`.

## GLB

**Expansion**

Binary glTF three-dimensional interchange asset.

**Does not mean**

An executable test, validated module or additional evidence by itself.
