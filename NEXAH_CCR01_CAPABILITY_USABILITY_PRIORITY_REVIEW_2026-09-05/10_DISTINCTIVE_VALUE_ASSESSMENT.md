# Distinctive Value Assessment

## Strongest evidenced combination

The most distinctive capability is not any single visual or algorithm. It is the bounded combination of:

```text
declared representations
→ explicit alignment/criterion
→ ILAU partition
→ preserved residual
→ ReturnTest
→ provenance and hashes
→ claim ceiling
→ Human confirmation or STOP
```

An ordinary comparison tool often produces a similarity score, diff or merged answer. The evidenced NEXAH combination can instead:

- identify information lost between two declared representations;
- separate retained material from representation-specific additions;
- keep unresolved differences visible rather than force consensus;
- preserve componentwise residuals rather than collapse them into one score;
- distinguish local agreement from global identity;
- bind a result to source identity, hashes, versions and a stated criterion;
- prevent a display consumer from becoming record owner;
- keep machine output separate from Human claim authority.

These are specific process properties. They do not establish better decisions, improved user outcomes or domain validity without external evaluation.

## Moderate or contextual distinctiveness

- OLS 1.0 provides coherent semantics and governance, but the value is specification discipline rather than a unique mathematical theory.
- Network Orientation and IEEE Geometry demonstrate useful bounded applications, but their algorithms use known graph, numerical and geometric methods. Distinctiveness comes from provenance, scope and multi-view reporting, not new mathematics.
- NRRC's 14-type candidate has a useful processor-neutral separation of record owner, processor and display consumer. It remains a candidate and is not yet interoperable production infrastructure.
- The public Library and Living Atlas are valuable editorial orientation spaces. Their distinctiveness is curated relationship visibility, not automated inference.

## No evidenced distinctive value

No distinctive software value is established merely by:

- large artifact volume;
- neon/color treatment;
- recurring numbers or glyph resemblance;
- musical resonance language;
- OEIS matches;
- visual similarity across biological, physical or cosmological forms;
- an HTML animation with no input/output contract;
- a historical BTC result with no recoverable dedicated entry point;
- a schema test without a user-facing operation.

## Claim ceiling

The evidence supports a distinctive orientation and audit discipline. It does not support claims of universal ontology, autonomous scientific authority, general AI routing, superior decision quality, market value or externally validated usefulness.
