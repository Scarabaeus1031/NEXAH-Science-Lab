# Public explanation

General audience: NEXAH shows how one relation can remain checkable while its orientation or representation changes, with reconstruction keeping the human reviewer in control.

Technical collaborator: The prototype combines three deterministic examples: conjugate symmetry in a Julia escape grid, CRT projection and exact reconstruction across coprime registers, and fixed-width reversal with maximal digit complement. Each declares its state space, operator, invariant, inverse or reconstruction, and claim ceiling; no physical interpretation is inferred.

Mathematical statement: For each selected domain, a typed map changes a representation while a specified invariant or equivariance relation is tested, and correctness is decided by an explicit inverse, reconstruction map, or exhaustive equality check; this is a cross-demonstrator methodology, not a universal theorem.

Central formulation: "Representation changes. Relations may survive. Reconstruction decides." Status: supported as a cross-demonstrator methodology, not asserted as universal mathematics.
