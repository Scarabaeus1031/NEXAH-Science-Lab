# Final NEXAH Translation-Fidelity Literature Audit

## Executive conclusion

`NO_DISTINCT_CONCEPTUAL_NOVELTY_FOUND`

The broad result is established under the invariance–selectivity or
invariance–discriminability trade-off, information bottleneck/minimal
sufficiency, coarse-graining, and representation expressivity. The internal
work is best understood as an independently produced synthetic demonstration
and a possibly useful operational combination: separately report consistency
under admissible representation changes (`R`), discrimination of matched
structural counterfactuals (`D`), and the collisions that explain apparent
success.

No new theorem, invariant, physical principle, universal law, predictive result,
or validated application was found. The correct decision is
`GO_EXTERNAL_REPLICATION_WITH_REFRAMED_QUESTION`.

## Required answers

### 1. Is the broad robustness–information–discrimination trade-off already known?

**Yes.** Invariance/selectivity and invariance/discriminative-power traditions
state the same core problem; information theory explains it through compression
and retained task information.

### 2. Under what established terminology?

Invariance versus selectivity/discriminability; nuisance invariance; sufficient
and minimal representations; information bottleneck; rate–distortion;
coarse-graining/state aggregation; descriptor robustness versus distinctiveness;
and graph-representation expressivity or collision.

### 3. Which literature comes closest?

Varma and Ray (2007) is the closest conceptual statement. Anselmi, Rosasco, and
Poggio (2016) is the closest invariance/selectivity formulation. Zhao et al.
(2022) is the closest formal Pareto treatment. Achille and Soatto (2018) and the
information bottleneck supply the information-removal account. Geiger and
Temmel (2013) is closest to state-transition aggregation.

### 4. Is “robustness through information loss” already standard?

**Yes in substance.** A constant descriptor is the limiting example of perfect
invariance without discrimination; minimal representations and information
bottlenecks formalize selective information removal. The phrase itself need not
be standard for the idea to be established.

### 5. Is separating robustness from discrimination standard?

**Yes in substance.** Descriptor evaluation, invariance/selectivity theory, and
anti-collapse representation learning explicitly treat both properties.

### 6. Is the `R/D` two-axis operationalization already known?

The **two-axis idea is known**. The exact frozen formulas, counterfactual panel,
and reporting workflow were not located verbatim. They are a possible new
operationalization, not a new concept.

### 7. Is collision counting novel?

**No as a general idea.** Non-injectivity, indistinguishability, aliasing, and
representation collapse are standard. The explicit finite collision ledger is
a transparent implementation choice whose incremental utility remains to be
shown.

### 8. Is the Pareto framing novel?

**No.** Zhao et al. explicitly analyze a Pareto frontier for invariant
representation learning. NEXAH's finite plotted points may be a different
visualization, not a new framing.

### 9. Is count-rank representation novel?

**Not established either way.** Rank/ordinal summaries and transition
aggregation have extensive precedents, but the search did not locate the exact
tie-aware rank of a transition-count matrix used as this intermediate fidelity
certificate. Status: `NOT_ENOUGH_EVIDENCE`, not “novel.”

### 10. Is the state-sequence to transition-graph setting meaningfully different?

It is a **specific application substrate**, but not a new general problem.
Markov state aggregation, symbolic/coarse-grained dynamics, and transition
networks already study information retention under state-to-transition
representations. The particular frozen synthetic suite and decoder comparison
are different operationally.

### 11. Did NEXAH discover anything mathematically new?

**No mathematical novelty was established.** There is no new theorem,
invariant, bound, or proof of sufficiency/optimality.

### 12. Did NEXAH discover anything scientifically new?

No distinct new general phenomenon was established. The exact synthetic
measurements are new observations in their fixtures, but they instantiate a
known principle and have not been externally validated.

### 13. Did NEXAH independently reproduce a known principle?

**Yes, in a bounded internal sense.** Two synthetic decoder regimes reproduced
a qualified coarse-robust/rich-discriminating tendency. This is independent
internal implementation, not external-team replication, and the exact v0.7
hierarchy failed to replicate.

### 14. Is there a new methodological combination?

**Possibly.** The combined preregistered certificate ladder, admissible
representation transformations, matched structural counterfactuals, separate
`R/D`, and collision ledger was not found as one package. Its ingredients are
known; distinctiveness and usefulness require further evidence.

### 15. What is the narrowest defensible contribution?

A collision-aware dual-axis evaluation protocol for finite
state-sequence-to-transition-graph translations, accompanied by a bounded
synthetic reproduction of the known invariance–discriminability tension and an
unresolved count-rank candidate.

### 16. What should absolutely not be claimed?

Do not claim a new trade-off, theorem, mathematical/physical invariant,
universal law, inevitable monotone frontier, decoder-independent support
invariant, optimal/sufficient count-rank certificate, prediction, causality,
early warning, stability, risk, control, IEEE/PEGASE result, or external
replication.

### 17. Is a paper justified?

**Not yet as a conceptual-discovery paper.** A method/evaluation note may be
justified after external replication demonstrates incremental diagnostic value
and a fuller protocol-level literature search does not find an exact duplicate.

### 18. If yes, what kind of paper?

A conservative methods or benchmark note: transparent collision-aware
evaluation of translated graph summaries, with preregistration, negative
results, standard baselines, and no universal claims.

### 19. Is external replication still justified?

**Yes, with a reframed question.** It should test reliability and added value of
the protocol in independently authored pipelines—not rediscover the broad
trade-off.

### 20. What is the single next scientific action?

Freeze a revised external preregistration whose primary endpoint is whether the
collision-aware `R/D` protocol changes or improves certificate-selection
diagnosis relative to established baselines across independent decoders,
unknown state counts, and held-out transformations; include the count-rank
certificate as a falsifiable secondary question.

review. Absence of an exact protocol match is therefore not proof of novelty.

- **Operationalization/protocol:** possibly distinctive; not yet externally
