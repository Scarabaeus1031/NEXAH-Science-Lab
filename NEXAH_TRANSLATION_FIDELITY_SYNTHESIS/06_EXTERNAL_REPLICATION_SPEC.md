# External Replication Specification

Status: specification only. Do not execute internally.

## Independence requirements

An external team should freeze and implement:

- a decoder written without canonical NEXAH or either internal runner;
- independently designed sequential/dynamical families and matched structural
  counterfactuals;
- multiple state-count assumptions, including unknown/misspecified counts;
- explicit sample → latent state → cluster → graph-node correspondence;
- multiple preregistered noise magnitudes;
- multiple delay depths and alignment conventions;
- multiple moderate and aggressive sampling maps;
- faithful and lossy transformations justified before results;
- separate robustness and discrimination metrics;
- an ordered certificate/detail ladder with transparent collision counts;
- frozen HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW and HIGH/HIGH criteria;
- deterministic seeds, complete error retention, hashes and independent replay;
- a decision rule that cannot be changed after inspection.

At least two decoder families should be included so decoder dependence is
estimable rather than narrated. The external investigators should design the
fixtures and transformations without reusing the exact internal motifs.

## Preregistered interpretation outcomes

### A. Strengthen the candidate

The candidate is strengthened if, across independent decoders and fixture
families, richer operational detail is consistently associated with lower R
and higher D; coarse-summary collisions explain substantial missed
counterfactuals; and at least one count-aware intermediate repeatedly lies on
the Pareto frontier. Exact internal rates need not recur.

### B. Weaken the candidate

It is weakened if association directions are mixed or near zero, occur only in
one decoder/transformation class, disappear under unknown state count, or
cannot be connected to measured certificate collisions. A HIGH/HIGH
certificate in some settings weakens any claim that the trade-off is generally
unavoidable, while remaining scientifically valuable.

### C. Falsify the broader hypothesis for the external protocol

Reject the broader candidate in that preregistered program if richer,
non-saturated certificates consistently match or exceed coarse certificates on
both R and D across the independent decoders, detail–robustness is nonnegative,
detail–discrimination is nonpositive, and collision/information-removal counts
do not explain robustness. A reproducible nontrivial HIGH/HIGH certificate that
Pareto-dominates the coarse layers would be especially strong falsification of
an unavoidable trade-off interpretation.

External results should be reported whether they strengthen, weaken or reject
the candidate. This is the point at which internal translation experimentation
stops.
