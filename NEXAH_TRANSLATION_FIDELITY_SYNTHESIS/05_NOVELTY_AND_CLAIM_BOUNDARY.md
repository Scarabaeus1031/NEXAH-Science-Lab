# Novelty and Claim Boundary

## Known / standard

- ordinary coordinate, phase, speed-offset and Euclidean isometry properties;
- graph relabeling and isomorphism facts;
- generic intuition that compression may improve robustness while discarding
  distinctions;
- standard dependence of clustering and learned partitions on representation,
  metric, sampling and hyperparameters;
- deterministic replay as a software-integrity check;
- Pareto analysis for two competing empirical objectives.

These are not discoveries of this research sequence.

## Possibly contributory

- a preregistered synthetic framework that measures translation robustness and
  matched structural discrimination as separate axes;
- an explicit certificate ladder from connectivity summaries through support,
  counts, ranks, bins and full probabilities;
- discrete collision/distinction accounting used to test whether robustness
  arises through information removal;
- explicit sample → source state → derived cluster → graph-node correspondence;
- independent-decoder replication of the broader pattern despite rejection of
  the exact original hierarchy;
- treating invariance as potentially lossy rather than presumptively truthful.

Every item in this section is classified:

`POTENTIAL_METHODOLOGICAL_CONTRIBUTION_REQUIRES_LITERATURE_REVIEW`

No novelty claim is made without a systematic literature review covering
representation stability, clustering stability, graph summarization,
invariant/equivariant representations, information bottlenecks, change
detection and multi-objective/Pareto evaluation.

## Claim boundary

The admissible claim is limited to reproducible behavior on the frozen
synthetic studies and their two internal implementations. “NEXAH” is historical
provenance for Studies 1–2, not the scientific subject of the general question.
The evidence does not justify domain transfer, utility, causal interpretation,
physical meaning or universal terminology.
