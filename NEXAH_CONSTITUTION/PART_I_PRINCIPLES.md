# Part I — Principles

Status: `ADOPTED WITHOUT MODIFICATION`

## NEXAH Authority Constitution

### Article 1 — Supremacy of Handlungshoheit

Handlungshoheit is the highest constitutional principle of NEXAH.

It is the inseparable combination of:

- decision authority;
- responsibility;
- intentional action;
- accountability.

Handlungshoheit belongs exclusively to the Human.

No software module, model, workflow, application, automated process or generated artifact may possess, inherit or simulate Handlungshoheit.

Software may support Human action. It may not become the constitutional actor.

### Article 2 — Bounded authority

Every architectural authority shall have:

- one declared purpose;
- one bounded scope;
- one identifiable owner;
- explicit exclusions;
- explicit dependencies;
- a defined artifact class.

No authority exists merely because software can perform an operation.

### Article 3 — Exclusivity

The same constitutional authority shall not be vested in two architectural responsibilities.

Adjacent responsibilities may process the same source only when their authority scopes remain different.

Shared access is not shared authority.

### Article 4 — Non-transfer

Authority may be referenced, consumed and relied upon.

Authority is never transferred by:

- consumption;
- copying;
- translation;
- rendering;
- dependency;
- execution order;
- repository placement;
- technical integration.

A downstream artifact retains only the authority explicitly assigned to its producer.

### Article 5 — Semantic authority

OLS is the sole semantic authority.

No downstream responsibility may redefine, repair, extend or replace OLS meaning implicitly.

A local explanation or application label does not become canonical terminology through use.

### Article 6 — Recognition authority

IRIS owns recognition and produces Recognition Records.

Recognition records what was detected, selected, framed, masked or left uncertain.

Recognition is not:

- truth;
- importance;
- semantic meaning;
- structural certification;
- domain validation;
- permission to act.

### Article 7 — Structural authority

ORION owns structural orientation.

ORION may validate and certify only structure admitted by its declared scope.

ORION certification does not establish semantic truth, empirical validity, domain validity, Human approval or permission to act.

### Article 8 — Translation authority

Translation Authority governs fidelity, not meaning.

Every translation shall preserve:

- source identity;
- source authority;
- mapping identity;
- declared loss;
- uncertainty;
- exclusions;
- provenance.

A translation that cannot preserve these properties shall fail explicitly.

### Article 9 — Presentation and application authority

SIRIUS owns the shared application-facing boundary.

Applications own their concrete interfaces and workflows.

Neither presentation nor application use changes upstream authority.

Applications may recommend. They may not decide on behalf of the Human.

### Article 10 — Domain authority

Domain Authority governs conclusions only within a named domain, method and evidence boundary.

Structural conformance and semantic conformance do not substitute for domain validation.

A domain conclusion cannot silently become an ecosystem-wide claim.

### Article 11 — Governance authority

Governance Authority establishes custody, adoption, versioning, freezes, exceptions and change procedures.

Governance may determine which authority is recognized. It may not manufacture scientific truth by declaration.

Every governance act requiring judgment must identify the Human exercising Handlungshoheit.

### Article 12 — Scope-relative validation

No validation is universal.

Every validation or certification statement shall identify:

- the validating authority;
- the object validated;
- the applicable scope;
- the evidence;
- the exclusions;
- the result.

An artifact valid in one scope remains unvalidated outside that scope.

### Article 13 — Explicit Human decision

A Human decision exists only when an identified Human intentionally records or communicates that decision under an applicable mandate.

The following do not constitute a Human decision:

- silence;
- continued software execution;
- a default option;
- a recommendation;
- a generated approval;
- an inferred preference;
- absence of rejection.

### Article 14 — Authority changes

Authority may change only through an explicit constitutional decision.

Authority shall never migrate through implementation convenience, repeated use, technical dependency, terminology drift or historical accident.
