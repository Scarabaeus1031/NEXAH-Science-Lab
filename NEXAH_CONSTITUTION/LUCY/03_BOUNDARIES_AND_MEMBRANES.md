# Boundaries and Membranes

Status: `CONSTITUTIONAL PREAMBLE`

## Boundary

A boundary distinguishes one thing, responsibility, context or world from
another.

Without boundaries, identity and accountability become ambiguous. A boundary
makes it possible to say:

- what is inside a scope;
- what remains outside;
- what may cross;
- under whose authority;
- with what loss or consequence.

## Wall

A wall maximizes separation.

Walls may be necessary for protection, confidentiality or safety. When treated
as the only form of boundary, they can isolate sources, prevent correction and
make coordination impossible.

## Membrane

A membrane separates while permitting bounded exchange.

It preserves identity without requiring isolation. It makes crossings visible
and conditional. What passes through may be selected, transformed, delayed or
rejected.

The membrane is used here as a constitutional model. It does not introduce a
scientific operator or biological claim.

## Healthy boundaries

Healthy systems require boundaries that are:

- explicit enough to preserve identity;
- permeable enough to permit relation;
- selective enough to protect integrity;
- observable enough to support accountability;
- revisable enough to respond to evidence and change.

A rigid wall may protect while preventing learning.

An absent boundary may permit exchange while dissolving responsibility and
identity.

## Dynamic boundaries

A living boundary may change, but it does not change silently.

The conditions of exchange must remain visible. A boundary revision must state
what changed, who authorized it and what consequences follow.

This principle applies to Human relations, knowledge practices and
constitutional authority without making them identical.

## Orientation at the boundary

Orientation is required before crossing a boundary and while interpreting what
has crossed it.

The orienting questions are:

1. What is being distinguished?
2. What may cross?
3. What is transformed or lost?
4. Which authority governs the crossing?
5. What uncertainty remains?
6. Who retains responsibility?

Orientation lives within dynamic boundaries because relation without
distinction is confusion, while distinction without relation is isolation.
