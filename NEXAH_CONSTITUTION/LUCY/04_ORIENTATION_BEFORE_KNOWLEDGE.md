# Orientation Before Knowledge

Status: `CONSTITUTIONAL PREAMBLE`

## Principle

Before knowledge can guide action, the Human must be oriented toward its
source, scope, meaning, uncertainty and consequence.

Knowledge alone does not determine what a Human should do. Orientation relates
knowledge to a body, environment, history, purpose, responsibility and possible
action.

## Orientation sequence

The following sequence describes increasing forms of mediation in Human
orientation:

```text
Form
↓
Body
↓
Biology
↓
Environment
↓
Relations
↓
Patterns
↓
Language
↓
Mathematics
↓
Science
↓
Technology
↓
Architecture
↓
Applications
```

### Form

Difference becomes perceptible through shape, change, contrast and continuity.

### Body

Perception is situated in a finite body with position, scale, limits and needs.

### Biology

The body participates in living processes that condition attention,
capability, vulnerability and adaptation.

### Environment

Life occurs within surroundings that offer possibilities, constraints and
unknowns.

### Relations

Things become orientable through their relations: near and far, before and
after, part and whole, cause and coincidence, self and other.

### Patterns

Repeated or organized relations may be recognized as patterns. Recognition is
provisional until its boundary and evidence are known.

### Language

Language makes distinctions communicable. It also carries ambiguity, history
and interpretation.

### Mathematics

Mathematics permits exact definitions, relations and derivations within
declared assumptions.

### Science

Science connects questions, observations, measurements, models, criticism and
revision through accountable methods.

### Technology

Technology turns selected knowledge and capability into instruments and
operations.

### Architecture

Architecture assigns boundaries, responsibilities and relations so that
technical capability remains governable.

### Applications

Applications return technical capability to concrete Human situations, where
use again requires judgment and responsibility.

## Interpretation boundary

This sequence is not:

- a theory of nature;
- a universal law of cognition;
- a developmental hierarchy;
- a claim that later stages are superior;
- an OLS operator sequence;
- a runtime pipeline.

Feedback occurs in every direction. Science changes perception. Technology
changes environments. Applications change culture. Bodies and relations change
what can be learned.

The sequence is a theory of Human Orientation: a reminder that every advanced
representation remains connected to embodied life and returns to Human
responsibility.
