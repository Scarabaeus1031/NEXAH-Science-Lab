# Worlds

Status: `CONSTITUTIONAL PREAMBLE`

## Reading rule

The worlds named here are overlapping perspectives on Human existence. They
are not a complete ontology, scientific taxonomy or architectural stack.

None completely contains another. None can replace Reality. Their boundaries
are relational and may change with context.

## Reality

Reality is what exists independently of any single Human, representation,
theory or system.

Every observation and model addresses only a bounded relation to Reality.

## Life

Life is the world of living processes, organisms, dependence, development,
adaptation and mortality.

Human life belongs to this world but does not exhaust it.

## Human

The Human world is embodied, experiential, relational, historical and
cultural. It includes the capacity to perceive, learn, speak, decide, act and
accept responsibility.

The Human is neither outside Reality nor reducible to a technical description.

## Eigenwelt

Eigenwelt is the lived world of the individual self: personal history,
identity, values, commitments and the orientation from which a person acts.

It is not isolated from body, society or environment.

## Innenwelt

Innenwelt is the experienced interior: bodily condition, sensation, affect,
memory, imagination and thought.

It is accessible imperfectly to the person and never completely available to
an external system.

## Umwelt

Umwelt is the meaningful surrounding world encountered by a living being from
its particular capacities and situation.

It is not identical to the total physical environment. It is the environment
as available for perception, relation and action.

## Mitwelt

Mitwelt is the world shared with others.

It includes communication, cooperation, conflict, care, institutions,
expectations and mutual responsibility. It is where individual action acquires
social consequence.

## Culture

Culture is the inherited and changing world of practices, symbols, languages,
values, arts, institutions and collective memory.

Culture shapes orientation without becoming a complete authority over Reality
or the individual Human.

## Knowledge

Knowledge is the organized result of inquiry, experience, evidence,
interpretation, criticism and revision.

Knowledge may be reliable without being complete. Its claims remain bounded by
their evidence, methods and domain.

## Technology

Technology extends Human capacities to observe, calculate, preserve,
communicate and act.

It also changes conditions, distributes effects and creates new dependencies.
Technology remains an instrument within the Human world. Capability does not
create legitimacy or Handlungshoheit.

## Applications

Applications place selected knowledge and technical capability into a concrete
Human context.

They mediate use. They do not inherit the authority of every source they
consume, and they do not own the decisions made through them.

## Boundary orientation

Orientation becomes necessary where these worlds meet:

- Reality and observation;
- body and environment;
- self and others;
- experience and language;
- culture and evidence;
- knowledge and uncertainty;
- technology and responsibility;
- application and Human decision.

Orientation does not collapse these distinctions. It helps the Human remain
aware of them while acting across them.
