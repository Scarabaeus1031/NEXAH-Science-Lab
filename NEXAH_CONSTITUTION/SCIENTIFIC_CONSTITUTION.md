## Constitutional scope

The [Ecosystem Constitution](README.md) governs authority and organization. The
Scientific Constitution governs scientific interpretation and methodological
discipline. The two documents are complementary and have different scopes.
This document does not create a hierarchy between those scopes and acquires no
organizational authority through its location in the Constitution package.

This document defines scientific direction. It does not automatically create a
Research Program, Translation Engine, Knowledge Graph, domain-specific
language, algebra, software package, operator, Comparison Card, experiment,
website page or publication. Any such implementation requires a separate,
bounded mandate.

## Preamble

NEXAH is founded on a simple scientific observation: knowledge is never
encountered without representation.

NEXAH studies not only scientific objects, but also the transformations through
which scientific objects become knowable across representations.

A phenomenon may be expressed as measurement, equation, diagram, simulation,
dataset, model, explanation, decision rule, software object or institutional
record. Each representation makes some relations accessible and leaves others
inaccessible. Each transformation between representations preserves certain
structures, removes others, introduces method-dependent artifacts, and leaves
some questions unresolved.

NEXAH exists to make these consequences explicit.

It does not ask that representations be treated as interchangeable. It provides
an architecture for determining how they differ, under which conditions they
can be compared, and which conclusions their evidence supports.

This Constitution defines the enduring scientific direction of NEXAH. It is
independent of any particular implementation, repository, operator vocabulary,
experiment, visual language or software system.

It governs scientific interpretation. It does not, by itself, validate a
scientific result, promote exploratory work to evidence or confer
organizational authority.

## 1. Purpose

NEXAH exists to improve orientation across representations.

Scientific and technical work depends on translations:

- observations become records;
- records become measurements;
- measurements become models;
- models become visualizations;
- mathematical structures become software;
- experiments become reports;
- reports become decisions;
- decisions become procedures;
- procedures become institutional practice.

These translations are necessary, but they are not neutral. They involve
choices of scale, boundary, resolution, terminology, method, purpose and
admissible evidence.

The scientific problem addressed by NEXAH is therefore:

> How can representations be translated and compared without hiding the
> assumptions, losses, artifacts, uncertainty and limits introduced by the
> translation?

Many established disciplines address parts of this problem. Mathematics
studies structure-preserving maps. Computer science studies types, semantics,
abstraction, transformation and interoperability. Systems engineering studies
views, interfaces, requirements, verification and traceability. Knowledge
representation studies concepts, relations, ontologies and information
exchange. Scientific methodology studies evidence, reproducibility and limits
of inference.

NEXAH does not replace these disciplines. It provides an orientation
architecture in which their relevant methods can be applied together without
erasing their distinctions.

The gap NEXAH seeks to fill is not a missing universal theory. It is a missing
discipline of explicit translation:

- between domain models;
- between scientific and computational representations;
- between evidence and interpretation;
- between local results and possible applications;
- between what is shown and what remains unknown.

Its purpose is to make these transitions inspectable, testable, reproducible
and bounded.

## 2. What NEXAH Is

### 2.1 An orientation architecture

Orientation is the disciplined determination of:

- what is being examined;
- from which position or representation;
- under which assumptions;
- for which purpose;
- with which evidence;
- within which limits;
- and through which valid continuation paths.

Orientation precedes navigation. Before selecting a path, a system must
identify its state, available representations, constraints, uncertainties and
goals.

NEXAH supports this process without claiming to replace human judgment.

### 2.2 A translation framework

NEXAH describes translations between typed representations.

A translation may connect:

- a phenomenon and a measurement;
- a trajectory and a recorded trace;
- a mathematical model and an implementation;
- a dataset and a visualization;
- two scientific models;
- an experiment and a report;
- a report and an application;
- a technical finding and an institutional decision.

A translation is valid only within declared conditions. Its validity does not
imply that source and target are identical. Translation does not imply equivalence.

### 2.3 A research grammar

NEXAH provides a disciplined order for scientific work:

> Question  
> Scope  
> Objects  
> Definitions  
> Assumptions  
> Protocol  
> Transformation or experiment  
> Results  
> Reproduction  
> Comparison  
> Evidence  
> Claim boundary  
> Review  
> Disposition

This grammar does not prescribe one method for every field. It establishes the
minimum structure required for work to be inspectable.

Domain-specific tools remain domain-specific.

### 2.4 A representation architecture

NEXAH treats representations as scientific objects.

A representation has:

- a source;
- a declared purpose;
- a domain of validity;
- a construction method;
- a resolution and scale;
- an information capacity;
- characteristic artifacts;
- and limits on reconstruction.

No representation is assumed to contain the whole of the object it represents.

### 2.5 An evidence-aware comparison system

NEXAH distinguishes between:

- an observed relation;
- a computed result;
- a reproducible result;
- an interpretation;
- a hypothesis;
- an analogy;
- an application;
- and an unsupported extension.

Comparison must retain these distinctions.

The architecture is evidence-aware because it records not only a conclusion,
but also the conditions under which that conclusion was produced and the
limits beyond which it may not be transferred.

### 2.6 Why these functions belong together

Orientation requires alternatives to be compared.

Comparison requires representations to be translated into a common or related
basis.

Translation requires explicit definitions of source, target, mapping and
preserved structure.

Those definitions require a research grammar.

The results require evidence, provenance, uncertainty and claim boundaries.

Therefore orientation, translation, representation, comparison and evidence
are not separate ambitions. They are interdependent parts of one scientific
architecture.

## 3. What NEXAH Is Not

NEXAH is not a new physical theory.

It does not propose hidden forces, undiscovered particles, universal fields,
privileged cosmological positions or new laws of nature.

NEXAH is not a replacement for mathematics.

It does not replace category theory, geometry, topology, statistics,
dynamical systems, logic, information theory or any other established
mathematical discipline.

NEXAH is not a universal ontology.

It does not claim that mathematics, software, biology, physics, language,
cognition and governance contain identical objects or operate through one
mechanism.

NEXAH is not evidence that recurring terminology identifies a universal
structure.

The recurrence of words such as relation, transition, boundary, trace, return,
memory, field or navigation is insufficient to establish a shared mathematical
object.

NEXAH is not a license for unrestricted cross-domain transfer.

A structure observed in one domain cannot be transferred to another without a
declared mapping and domain-appropriate evidence.

NEXAH does not equate:

- similarity with identity;
- analogy with mechanism;
- correlation with causation;
- recurrence with universality;
- stability in a model with stability in nature;
- a controlled result with free behaviour;
- a visualization with an explanation;
- a record with its source;
- mathematical possibility with physical reality;
- physical possibility with institutional permission.

A record is not its source.

Governance requires separate authority.

NEXAH makes no unsupported claims concerning cosmology, biology, psychology,
consciousness or human destiny.

Patterns generate hypotheses, not conclusions.

## 4. The Core Research Question

The central research question of NEXAH is:

> Under what declared mappings can structures be translated between different
> representations while preserving evidence, limitations, relevant meaning
> and the conditions of valid interpretation?

This question contains several subordinate questions:

1. What are the source and target objects?
2. What representation types are involved?
3. Which structures are intended to correspond?
4. What mapping performs or proposes the translation?
5. Which relations remain invariant?
6. Which distinctions disappear?
7. Which structures are introduced by the method?
8. Which questions cannot be resolved from the result?
9. Which assumptions are required?
10. What evidence supports the correspondence?
11. At what scale and for which purpose is it valid?
12. Can the translation be independently reproduced?
13. Can translations be composed without changing their meaning?
14. Which conclusions remain valid after composition?
15. What observation would falsify the proposed correspondence?

The phrase “the same structure” is never accepted without qualification.

Sameness may mean identity, equivalence, similarity, correspondence, invariant
preservation, analogy, homomorphism, isomorphism, simulation or bisimulation.
These are not interchangeable.

The strength of every conclusion must be limited to the strength of the
demonstrated mapping.

## 5. Translation as the Central Scientific Discipline

Translation in NEXAH is broader than linguistic translation.

It is any declared transformation through which an object, relation, process or
result becomes available in another representation.

### 5.1 Mathematics and software

A mathematical definition translated into software may lose generality,
introduce numerical approximation, require finite precision or add
implementation-dependent state.

The implementation must not be treated as identical to the mathematical object
merely because it uses the same name.

### 5.2 Geometry and visualization

A geometric object translated into a diagram or projection preserves selected
relations while distorting or omitting others.

A visualization is an instrument of access. It is not the object itself.

### 5.3 Dynamical systems and records

An evolving system becomes scientifically available through finite
observations, sampled trajectories, endpoints, statistics or event records.

The record depends on sampling, thresholds, duration, controller conditions,
noise assumptions and observation protocols.

### 5.4 Experiments and reports

An experiment is translated into tables, figures, summaries, interpretations
and claims.

Each stage may compress information. Reproducible outputs and provenance are
necessary to keep the report connected to the run that produced it.

### 5.5 Governance and authorization

Evidence may inform governance, but representation is not authorization.

A scientific result does not automatically change policy, status, authority or
institutional obligation. Governance requires separate procedures,
responsibilities and review.

### 5.6 Documentation and education

Scientific knowledge is translated into documentation and educational
material. Clarity may require simplification, but simplification must not
silently strengthen the claim.

### 5.7 Human and machine reasoning

Humans and computational systems may organize the same evidence differently.
Translation between them requires explicit schemas, terminology, provenance,
uncertainty and reviewable outputs.

Translation becomes an object of research because its effects can be measured:

- preservation;
- distortion;
- reconstruction;
- interoperability;
- reader understanding;
- error propagation;
- decision relevance;
- and unsupported inference.

## 6. Structural Identity

Future NEXAH research shall treat structural identity as a declared and
testable relation.

The following terms must remain distinct.

### Equivalence

Two objects are equivalent under a specified criterion, even if they are not
identical.

The criterion must be named.

### Similarity

Two objects share selected features according to a declared measure.

Similarity may be graded and representation-dependent.

### Correspondence

Elements or relations in one representation are paired with elements or
relations in another.

A correspondence does not necessarily preserve operations.

### Invariant

A declared property remains unchanged under a specified transformation.

An invariant always belongs to a transformation and a domain of validity.

### Analogy

A relational organization in one domain provides a limited model for reasoning
about another.

Analogy supports hypothesis formation, not automatic transfer.

### Homomorphism

A mapping preserves specified operations or relations.

What is preserved must be stated explicitly.

### Isomorphism

A reversible structure-preserving mapping exists under a declared
formalization.

Isomorphism within a model does not prove mechanistic identity between domains.

### Simulation

One declared system reproduces specified behaviours of another under a stated
relation and observation criterion.

Simulation does not establish equality of implementation, mechanism or domain
meaning.

### Bisimulation

Two state-based systems can match each other’s declared transitions or
observable behaviours under a specified relation.

Bisimulation concerns behaviour under a model. It does not establish equality
of material realization or meaning.

Structural identity becomes measurable when the framework declares:

- the objects being compared;
- the feature or relation universe;
- the mapping;
- the preservation conditions;
- permitted tolerances;
- failure criteria;
- and the evidence supporting the classification.

Intuitive resemblance may initiate an investigation. It cannot complete one.

## 7. Translation Contracts

Every scientific translation in NEXAH shall be describable through a
Translation Contract.

A Translation Contract is an auditable record. It is not evidence merely
because it exists.

At minimum, it declares:

### Source

The originating object, representation, dataset, model, record or domain.

### Target

The representation or domain produced or addressed by the translation.

### Purpose and scope

The question the translation is intended to support and the limits within
which it is applicable.

### Assumptions

The conditions taken as given, including scale, units, sampling,
normalization, boundary conditions and domain conventions.

### Mapping

The formula, algorithm, procedure, relation, interpretation rule or human
method connecting source and target.

### Preserved structures

The features, relations, constraints or behaviours demonstrated to remain
valid.

### Discarded structures

Information removed, collapsed, masked, aggregated or made
non-reconstructable.

### Introduced artifacts

Structures created by the representation, cut, controller, approximation,
projection, interface or documentation method.

### Uncertainty

What remains unresolved, insufficiently sampled, untested, disputed or
non-reconstructable.

### Evidence

The observations, derivations, tests, experiments, validations or independent
assessments supporting the contract.

### Provenance

The origin, version, authorship, inputs, parameters, code, execution records
and dependencies required to audit the result.

### Confidence

A bounded assessment tied to declared evidence, not a rhetorical expression of
certainty.

### Failure and insufficiency states

The conditions under which the translation is invalid, undefined, numerically
unstable, unsupported or unable to return a scientific result.

### Claim boundary

The strongest interpretation permitted by the evidence and the explicit claims
that remain prohibited.

Translation Contracts shall be versioned and reproducible where execution is
involved.

Every translation must leave an audit trail sufficient to determine:

- what was transformed;
- by which method;
- under which conditions;
- with which result;
- and with which limitations.

## 8. Evidence Philosophy

NEXAH adopts the following scientific discipline.

### Evidence before assertion

Claims must follow evidence. Narrative coherence is not a substitute for
measurement, derivation or validation.

### Transparency before elegance

A transparent limited model is preferable to an elegant model whose
assumptions or transformations cannot be inspected.

### Reproducibility before complexity

Complexity is justified only when simpler reproducible methods are
insufficient.

### Declared assumptions

Assumptions, cuts, thresholds, controllers, normalizations, priors and
exclusions must be recorded.

### Bounded claims

Every conclusion is limited to its evidence class, protocol, domain, scale and
representation.

### Independent verification

Important results should be reproducible outside the editorial or
implementation lineage that produced them.

### Unknowns are first-class citizens

`UNKNOWN`, `NOT TESTED`, `INSUFFICIENT` and `NON-RECONSTRUCTABLE` are valid
scientific outcomes.

They must not be replaced by speculation.

### Negative results are retained

Failure to reproduce, failure to translate, lack of added value and rejection
of an analogy are meaningful results.

### Models remain models

A model may be useful, predictive or internally coherent without being
ontologically complete.

### Controlled and uncontrolled behaviour remain distinct

A result produced under intervention, target bias, normalization, filtering or
control must not be reported as free-system behaviour.

A controlled result is not a free-system result.

### Promotion is never automatic

Exploration does not automatically become Research. Research does not
automatically become Evidence. Evidence does not automatically become
Application, Publication, Governance or truth.

No promotion is automatic.

### STOP is a scientific disposition

Work may stop when:

- the bounded question has been answered;
- the instrument has been validated;
- the proposed mapping has failed;
- the result offers no improvement over a simpler baseline;
- or additional interpretation is not supported.

STOP is a valid scientific disposition. Stopping preserves scientific discipline.

## 9. Research Directions

The following are long-term research directions, not automatically adopted
Programs. Their activation requires bounded questions, ownership, methods and
evidence criteria.

### Translation Research

Study the behaviour of mappings between representations, including
preservation, loss, artifacts, uncertainty, reconstruction and composition.

### Representation Research

Study how scale, projection, sampling, masks, interfaces, aggregation and
visualization affect what can be observed and inferred.

### Structural Identity

Develop and test explicit criteria for equivalence, correspondence,
simulation, analogy, invariant preservation and behavioural matching.

### Scientific Grammars

Study whether typed documentation and operator contracts improve
reproducibility, independent reconstruction, interoperability and boundary
recognition.

### Evidence Engineering

Develop methods for connecting definitions, implementations, experiments,
results, provenance, confidence and claim boundaries.

### Orientation Systems

Study how humans and computational systems select representations and
continuation paths under uncertainty and purpose constraints.

### Cross-domain Mapping

Investigate limited mappings between established domain models without
assuming shared mechanisms.

Cross-domain work must include strong negative controls and domain review.

### Navigation of Knowledge

Study how researchers, engineers, readers and AI systems move through
heterogeneous evidence without losing source identity, uncertainty or
authority boundaries.

These directions shall extend established scientific and engineering
disciplines rather than compete with them.

## 10. Future Vision

If successful, NEXAH may become a durable research infrastructure for
evidence-aware interoperability.

It may provide:

- a shared language for declaring translations;
- tools for comparing scientific representations;
- executable translation contracts;
- reproducible evidence packages;
- provenance-preserving knowledge systems;
- methods for detecting unsupported transfer;
- interfaces between mathematical definitions and software implementations;
- structured communication between researchers, engineers, institutions,
  humans and AI systems.

NEXAH may help make heterogeneous models comparable without forcing them into
a single ontology.

It may help scientific software communicate not only outputs, but also
assumptions, transformation histories, failure states and claim boundaries.

It may help educational and public-facing systems simplify scientific material
without concealing what has been removed or introduced.

It may help AI systems distinguish:

- evidence from interpretation;
- analogy from equivalence;
- model output from physical observation;
- repository status from scientific status;
- recommendation from authorization.

Success will not be measured by the number of concepts, operators, diagrams or
domains incorporated.

It will be measured by whether NEXAH enables:

- more reliable translation;
- fewer category errors;
- better independent reconstruction;
- clearer uncertainty;
- stronger reproducibility;
- more appropriate model selection;
- and more disciplined conclusions.

NEXAH shall remain replaceable by better methods. It must not protect its own
terminology from comparison, criticism, simplification or rejection.

## 11. Constitutional Principles

1. **Evidence before assertion.**
2. **Orientation precedes navigation.**
3. **Translation precedes comparison.**
4. **Declare the source and the target.**
5. **Declare assumptions, cuts, scales and conditions.**
6. **Preserve provenance.**
7. **Distinguish the system from its representation.**
8. **Distinguish the record from its source.**
9. **Every representation has limits.**
10. **Every nontrivial translation may preserve, discard, introduce and leave
    unresolved information.**
11. **Unknowns are scientific results.**
12. **Similarity is not identity.**
13. **Analogy is not mechanism.**
14. **Mathematical equivalence is not automatic physical, biological,
    linguistic or institutional equivalence.**
15. **Patterns generate hypotheses, not conclusions.**
16. **A controlled result is not a free-system result.**
17. **Composition requires compatible types and preserved meaning.**
18. **Claims shall not exceed their evidence.**
19. **Exploration, Research, Evidence, Application and Governance remain
    distinct.**
20. **No promotion is automatic.**
21. **Prefer reproducible simplicity to unreproducible complexity.**
22. **Retain negative results and failed translations.**
23. **Stop when the bounded question is answered or the stronger claim is
    unsupported.**
24. **Do not ask people to believe. Ask them to inspect, reproduce, compare and
    decide.**

## Closing Declaration

NEXAH is an architecture for working responsibly between representations.

It does not promise a view from nowhere. It requires every view to declare its
position, construction, evidence and limits.

It does not seek to collapse disciplines into one language. It seeks to make
translations between them explicit enough to examine.

It does not convert patterns into truths. It converts proposed correspondences
into testable contracts.

Its enduring task is not to eliminate uncertainty, difference or plurality.

Its task is to make them scientifically navigable.
